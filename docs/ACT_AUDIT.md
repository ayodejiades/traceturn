# Act precision audit

Generated by `pnpm claim:verify` from the samples and labels in `evidence/act-audit-*.json`. Change a label and re-run to see the numbers move.

**Who labelled:** Claude, the model that wrote the act rules. These labels are not independent. A second annotator re-labelling `evidence/act-audit-labels-test.json` is the check that matters; every item carries a window of its source record and the episode's origin sentence.

## What is measured

Whether a detected act is a real act on the shared quantity: the agent submitted, posted, wrote or handed off *this* number, as the same quantity the episode is about. It does not measure whether the grade (grounded, ungrounded, after a correction) is right, except where the window itself shows the grade is wrong (codes YC and YT). A wrongly *grounded* act would need the other agents' turns to spot and is not audited.

Codes: Y real act; YC real act, but the window shows a check the grounding missed; YT real act, but it preceded the correction it was graded after; Q not the same quantity; N same number, not an act; U cannot tell (left out of every rate).

## Method

- `pnpm audit:acts` draws acts per corpus, evidence and grounding stratum in a fixed order (sha256 of salt, corpus, source record and claim) from the full act set, not the curated slice.
- The **dev set** (78 items) was labelled first. It led to one rule change: in an in-place `sed`, the pattern being replaced is not written.
- The **held-out set** (108 items) was drawn after the rules were frozen, with a different salt and excluding every dev item. Only its numbers describe the shipped rules.
- The sample oversamples the small strata (chat-reported and session-goal acts), so the weighted figures below are the ones that describe the corpus.

## What goes wrong

- **Q** (12 held-out items): Not the same quantity: the "quantity" is a name, id, title or a different amount (`311 data`, `805 poster`, `374 FALSE`).
- **N** (10 held-out items): The same number but not an act: the value being replaced, an audience or outcome, a game move, a read-only check.
- **YC** (4): the act was graded ungrounded although its own text or the origin sentence reports a check ("independently parsed", "matching live dashboard display", "latest bounded recheck"). Ungrounded counts are therefore an upper bound.
- **YT** (2 of 10 after-correction items): a wiki act is an edit that *reports* an answer, and its time is when the report was posted. Two reports say outright that the answer preceded the correction, and the rest cannot be told from the text. After-correction counts on the wiki may be overstated.
- The Q failures are the claim extractor treating a name or id as a quantity. They are not act errors, and fixing them would change every lineage, so it is left for a change that re-runs the whole pipeline and the classifier audit.
